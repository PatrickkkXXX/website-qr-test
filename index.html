[ What Just Happened? ]

You scanned a QR code -- possibly from a flyer, poster, or printed material. That is exactly how QR phishing (quishing) attacks work in the real world.

Attackers print or display malicious QR codes in physical spaces -- offices, cafes, parking meters, conference rooms. Victims scan them without questioning the source.

This was a safe, controlled demonstration run by Patrick Reyes to test and raise awareness. No data was stolen. But a real attacker could have redirected you to a fake login page to steal your credentials.

[ Red Flags to Watch For ]

  • QR code on a sticker covering the original
  • Unexpected URL after scanning
  • Urgency: "Scan to claim your prize!"
  • Request for login credentials or payment
  • QR in unsolicited emails or messages
  • No clear origin or branding on the code

[ Safe Habits ]

  • Preview the URL before opening it
  • Only scan codes from trusted sources
  • Use a QR scanner that shows the link first
  • When in doubt, type the URL manually
  • Report suspicious codes to IT/security
  • Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA)
